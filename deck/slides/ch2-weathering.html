CHAPTER 2 · WEATHERING

Break it or change it

Mechanical (physical)

Rock is broken into smaller pieces. Its composition does not change.

Agents: temperature swings, freeze–thaw, glaciers, wind, running water, waves.

Product: gravel, sand and silt.

Chemical

Minerals react and turn into new minerals.

Agents: water + CO₂ (carbonic acid), dissolved salts, organic acids.

Product: clay minerals, e.g. orthoclase → kaolinite.

Bowen’s series: olivine → augite → hornblende → biotite … orthoclase → muscovite → quartz. Top of the series weathers first; quartz is the most resistant.

Transported soils: glacial, alluvial (rivers), lacustrine (lakes), marine, aeolian (wind), colluvial (gravity). Residual soils stay where they formed.

Chapter 2 · Origin of soil

Mechanical weathering changes size only. Chemical weathering changes the mineral itself, and that is where clay minerals come from. Residual soil profile: fine at the surface, coarser with depth, angular rock fragments deeper down.
